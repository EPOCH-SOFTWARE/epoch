EPOCH prototype

Kept time.

The best living identities keep one thing fixed and let one real signal change it: Nordkyn’s logo is reshaped by the live weather, MIT Media Lab’s by an algorithm with 40,000 variations, the Whitney’s W by the space it has to fill. EPOCH’s signal is time. None of these put a picture in front of the name; time changes the logo itself. Seeded by the second you opened this page, .

New: 08–15 ↓

01

Now

The mark is a clock. Its opening always points to the current time of day, with the orange point sitting in it, so the logo is never the same twice. Shown here as a time-lapse: one day in 24 seconds.

On screen: moves with the real clock, almost too slowly to notice.In print: every business card and proposal carries the moment it was printed.

02

Daylight

The O is the sky. A horizon crosses it, and the orange sun rises and sets inside it with your local time. After sunset a small moon takes its place. Shown as a time-lapse: one day in 24 seconds.

Like Nordkyn: a real-world signal shapes the logo, so it is never wrong about the time of day.Belief: we work around the clock, and it shows.

03

Weight of the day

The letters gain weight with the light: a hairline at midnight, at their boldest at noon, easing back down by evening. The same name, never quite the same thickness twice. Time-lapse: one day in 24 seconds.

Like the WPP campus logo: one typeface whose weight is driven by the time of day.Belief: full strength when it matters most.

04

Timestamp

Under the name, 32 cells spell the current Unix time in binary, the count of seconds since the epoch on January 1, 1970. It ticks every second; the cells that just changed flash orange. Every logo is a real, unique moment.

Like MIT Media Lab: one system, endless variations, except every one is true.Use: stamp each proposal, contract and launch with its exact second.

05

Sundial

The name is the sundial. Its letters cast real shadows that swing and stretch with the sun: long at dawn, short at noon, gone at night. The orange point is the sun itself. Time-lapse: one day in 24 seconds.

Like a real sundial: you can read the hour from the logo.Belief: built for daylight, measured honestly.

06

Tonight’s moon

The O carries the real phase of the moon in orange, calculated from the lunar cycle, so tonight’s logo matches tonight’s sky. Over a month it waxes to full and wanes back to new. Shown here: one lunar month in 20 seconds.

Rhythm: a quiet change every night that people notice over weeks.Ambition: a brand in step with something much bigger than itself.

07

Twenty-four hours

Read the name left to right and you read the last day. Each thin slice of the letters is drawn at the strength of the light at that hour, so the logo becomes a chart of the past 24 hours, ending at the orange line: now.

Data, not decoration: every bump in the letters is a real hour.Belief: the record of the work is the work.

EPOCH or nothing.

Eight more marks where the logo itself keeps time. The time-lapses start at your local time.

08

In the name

The O in EPOCH is the clock. Its opening and the orange point turn with the time of day, like 01, so the name itself keeps time. Time-lapse: one day in 24 seconds.

One word: no separate symbol. The O alone is the app icon.Like logo 1 (the dot in the O), but alive.

09

Display

E, P, O, C and H are all letters a digital clock can show. So the name is written on clock segments, and at the turn of every minute it flips to the time, then back. The orange point never moves: it is the center of the O, then the colon.

Hidden in plain sight: the name and the time are made of the same seven strokes.Shown here every few seconds; in use, once a minute.

10

Overrun

One full turn, and then it keeps going. The line runs past its own start, and its orange tip points to the time of day. Time-lapse: one day in 24 seconds.

Belief: we don’t stop at the finish line.Round 1’s Overrun, now keeping time.

11

Hour and minute

A watch you can read. The opening with the orange point is the hour hand; the narrow cut is the minute hand. At twelve they meet at the top and it is the logo exactly. Shown here: one hour in 30 seconds.

Precise: read it to the minute, like any watch.Twice a day it lines up into the current logo.

12

Engraved

From across the room it is the logo. Up close, the ring is engraved with the exact second it was drawn: the date, the time and the count of seconds since 1970. Every copy is an original.

In print: read any business card with a loupe.The engraving starts with EPOCH OR NOTHING.

13

Full circle

The opening is what is left of today. It narrows hour by hour: at noon it is the current logo, and at midnight it closes into a full circle with the orange point on top. Then a new day opens. Time-lapse: one day in 24 seconds.

Belief: every day, we close the loop.At midnight: a 0 and a 1. EPOCH or nothing.

14

Totality

The mark as a total eclipse of the sun: a perfect ring of light with one bright point, the moment astronomers call the diamond ring. The bright side turns with the time of day. Time-lapse: one day in 24 seconds.

Exact: eclipses are timed to the second, centuries ahead.Belief: 99% is not totality. EPOCH or nothing.

15

On the minute

The orange point runs one lap of the ring every minute, then waits in the opening for the minute to turn, like the second hand of the Swiss railway clock. Real time.

Belief: on time, every time.At rest, it is the current logo, exactly.
